# hum-pitch baseline: existing note transcribers on humming (2026-11-23)

Measurement only, on CPU, nothing trained. Method and sets: [../README.md](../README.md). Every slice and the
per-group error split: [tables.md](tables.md) (generated by `scripts/score.py`); per-clip scores in
`/mnt/d/casperaki-data/hum-pitch/results/per_clip.csv`.

* **Real hums**: 296 HumTrans TEST recordings (7,826 notes), the score MIDI shifted by hum-beats' fitted
  per-recording time offset and moved to the octave the person hummed in.
* **Synthetic hums**: 500 test clips (10,463 notes), 250 from round one and 250 from round two, each 100 voice,
  50 breathy and 100 instrument leads; reference = INTENDED pitch at the PERFORMED onset and offset.
* **F onset**: mir_eval note F, onset +-50 ms and pitch +-50 cents (mean over clips). **F on+off**: offset too.
  **Right pitch**: share of reference notes with an estimated note of the same MIDI pitch starting within +-100 ms
  (pooled over notes). **Octave / semitone**: share of reference notes with an estimated note within +-100 ms that
  is a whole octave off / one semitone off. Every estimate is a whole MIDI note.
* Basic Pitch "tuned" = onset 0.9, frame 0.2, minimum note 180 ms, the best of 112 settings on 149 HumTrans
  TRAIN recordings (onset-only F 0.275 -> 0.369 there; [basic-pitch-tuning.csv](basic-pitch-tuning.csv)).

## What the numbers say

1. **No off-the-shelf model is good at humming.** The best gets about two notes in three right in pitch on real hums
   (SwiftF0 + segmentation 65.6%; women 79.7%, men 53.5%), and about half of the note onsets within 50 ms.
2. **Basic Pitch is the weakest on hums**, defaults or tuned. Its defaults fire too many onsets on a hummed voice
   (the tuning moved the onset threshold to the edge of the grid, 0.9); tuning raises onset F on real hums
   (0.262 -> 0.364) but loses notes (missed 28% -> 32%) and does worse on every synthetic slice, so the real-hum
   optimum does not carry over. It is at its best on library instruments, close to what it was trained on.
3. **Wrong pitches are semitone errors, not octave errors.** Of the notes with a wrong pitch, 73-93% are one
   semitone off on real hums and 0-7% an octave off (synthetic instruments: up to 20% octave). The hummer is out of
   tune (detune, slow drift) and rounding the sung pitch lands on the neighbour. A pitch tracker cannot fix that;
   a model that learns the INTENDED note can, which is what the synthetic labels provide.
4. **Men are much harder than women** (right pitch with pYIN or SwiftF0: 52-54% vs 75-80%; semitone errors 22% vs 7-8%).
5. **Round two is much harder than round one** (right pitch with pYIN or SwiftF0: 52-54% vs 72-76%): its pitch contours carry real-hum
   deviations, so its semitone errors (17-21%) look like the real hums (15%). Round one is too easy on pitch.
6. **Offsets are poor everywhere** (F on+off 0.09-0.39): legato hums, and repeated notes of one pitch merge in the
   simple segmentation.

## Caveats

* **HumTrans onset times run early.** On matched notes every model's onset is 27-45 ms after the HumTrans
  reference (synthetic: 2-18 ms). hum-beats fitted the HumTrans time offset to energy onsets (librosa, backtracked),
  which sit before the pitch settles. So HumTrans onset F understates every model, and most of all SwiftF0, whose
  onsets lag by another 15 ms (16 ms frames); that is why SwiftF0 is behind pYIN there on onset F but ahead on pitch.
  Right pitch (+-100 ms) is the fairer comparison on real hums.
* **HumTrans octave**: the score MIDI is in the score's octave; 125 of 158 men hum an octave below it, 3 two octaves
  below, 28 at it, 2 above; 2 of 138 women hum an octave above. References are moved by whole octaves
  (`scripts/fit_octaves.py`, measured with pYIN and SwiftF0, which agreed on every recording; none left out).
  Scored in the score octave, men's hums would count as octave errors.
* One HumTrans reference note (M03_0329_0002_1_DD) falls before the recording starts after the offset; left out.
* The segmentation for pYIN and SwiftF0 is deliberately simple and was not tuned; Basic Pitch got a 112-setting
  search. Basic Pitch is polyphonic and may emit overlapping notes; it was scored as it comes out.
* Round-two clips take their pitch deviations from HumTrans TRAIN recordings (`contour_source` in their JSON; none
  from TEST). HumTrans is CC BY-NC and was declared evaluation-only in hum-beats: using round two to TRAIN a note
  model is the owner's call.
